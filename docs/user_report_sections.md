# User Study Findings

The user study evaluated the system across five external participants representing varied levels of technical background, ordering familiarity, and accessibility awareness (including participants with software development and food service experience). Quantitative Likert evaluations demonstrated a strong overall baseline—average satisfaction was 4.40 / 5.00, ease of navigation was 4.60 / 5.00, and feature discoverability was 4.60 / 5.00. However, qualitative feedback revealed critical blind spots in business logic, menu discovery, cart management, and system feedback.

### Overall Usability and General Impressions

Participants universally praised the cleanliness, straightforward workflow, and semantic HTML accessibility foundation. Testers noted that navigating between different roles was intuitive and that product selection and basic ordering operations were easy to locate. Accessibility-conscious participants specifically commended the inclusion of built-in text resizing and high-contrast controls. The system was broadly characterized as approachable and functional for a casual bubble tea customer.

### Theme 1: Ingredient Customization Boundaries and Menu Logic (Blind Spot)

A primary blind spot uncovered during testing was the lack of separation between consumable recipe ingredients and physical packaging items in the drink customization interface. Three out of five participants explicitly noted that customers were permitted to deselect essential operational items such as cups, lids, and straws (e.g., placing an order with 'No Cup'). Participants with prior food service experience highlighted that packaging materials must not be customer-modifiable constituents, as this creates nonsensical kitchen orders and breaks real-world inventory tracking. Furthermore, users expressed confusion over which customization choices were required versus optional, noting the absence of pre-selected defaults for ice and sugar levels.

### Theme 2: Drink Catalog Organization and Cognitive Strain

While participants appreciated the breadth of menu offerings and available add-ins, multiple testers experienced friction when attempting to locate specific drinks. In earlier iterations, all menu items and add-in constituents were displayed in an unorganized, flat grid without category headers or search functionality. This presented substantial cognitive load, forcing users to visually scan through every card sequentially to find a desired drink (such as distinguishing fruit teas from milk teas) or topping. Participants indicated that categorizing the drinks and add-ins is essential for rapid kiosk decision-making.

### Theme 3: Cart Modification and Viewport Ergonomics

A major workflow hurdle occurred during the review phase: once a drink was customized and added to the cart, the system provided only a 'Remove' button with no mechanism to modify selections. Participants who wished to alter an ice level or add an omitted topping had to delete the entire item and reconstruct it from scratch. Additionally, on mobile and smaller tablet viewports, users reported that the order total and submission buttons were pushed below the fold beneath long lists of customizations, requiring excessive scrolling to verify prices and complete checkout.

### Theme 4: Cross-Interface Visual Cohesion and Navigation Predictability

Participants noted noticeable visual and structural disparity across the system's different views. While individual pages had appealing elements, the design language varied noticeably between the Kiosk menu, Cashier screen, and Kitchen dashboard. For instance, the button styles, background colors, and typography varied between the customization modal and the checkout screen. Navigation predictability was also inconsistent: several views lacked an obvious 'Back to Menu' or 'Return to Portal' escape route, forcing users to rely on the browser's native back button or leaving them unsure of how to return to the home screen.

### Theme 5: Kitchen Operational Maturity and Button Responsiveness

The Kitchen display screen was identified as the least mature component in the application. Testers observed that kitchen action buttons (such as status progression or ticket undo) did not produce visible UI feedback or state updates, leading users to believe the buttons were inactive or broken. Furthermore, tickets lacked a clear visual distinction between standard ingredients, customer-requested add-ins, and omitted items, making it difficult for kitchen staff to quickly interpret order tickets at a glance.

### Theme 6: Accessibility Control Safeguards

While the accessibility toolbar was well-received by participants, edge-case testing revealed a critical usability bug: reducing the font size to its minimum allowed setting caused all text content across the site to vanish entirely, breaking the interface. Testers noted that while scaling is vital for low-vision users, strict bounding limits must be enforced to ensure readability is never compromised.

# Proposed Design Moves

| High-level Design Move | Detailed Description | Justification |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Packaging & Constituent Whitelist Filtering** | Filter out all non-consumable packaging items (cups, lids, straws, napkins, sleeves) from the customer customization interface, exposing only consumable recipe ingredients (tea base, milk, sugar, ice, boba) for removal or addition. | Directly resolves the primary blind spot raised by Participants 1, 3, and 4; prevents invalid orders ('drink with no cup') and aligns POS behavior with commercial food-service standards. |
| **Menu Categorization, Search, & Sorting Controls** | Implement sticky category filter tabs (All, Milk Teas, Fruit & Green Teas, Specialties), a text-based live search input, and sorting dropdowns (price, name) on the Kiosk and Cashier menus. | Eliminates the severe cognitive overload and scanning friction reported by Participant 4, allowing users to rapidly find target beverages without hunting through an unorganized grid. |
| **In-Cart Item Modification** | Add a 'Modify' / 'Edit' action button alongside the 'Remove' option in both Kiosk checkout and Cashier cart views. Clicking it reopens the customization screen pre-populated with the item's current configuration, updating the cart entry upon confirmation. | Eliminates the critical user frustration identified by Participant 4, where customers had to completely delete and re-customize an item to make minor adjustments. |
| **Fixed Bottom Action Bar & Responsive Cart Summary** | Redesign the customization and ordering views to incorporate a fixed bottom footer (.order-bar) containing running item count, real-time subtotal calculation, 'Back to Menu', and primary 'Add to Order' / 'Save Changes' actions. | Prevents critical order totals and submission buttons from being pushed below the fold on mobile and tablet viewports, directly solving Participant 4's viewport scrolling issue. |
| **Interactive Kitchen Display System (KDS) & Status Tracking** | Upgrade kitchen tickets to clearly highlight added toppings in green, display omitted constituents with strikethroughs, and provide reactive status advancement (Pending → In Progress → Complete) with responsive, high-contrast action buttons. | Directly resolves feedback from Participants 3 and 5, who observed non-reactive kitchen buttons and a lack of granular modification visibility on order tickets. |
| **Kitchen Layout Masonry & Dynamic History Loading** | Transition the kitchen order board from a rigid, stretched CSS grid to a multi-column masonry flow where cards adapt strictly to ticket length. Replace hardcoded order limits with a 'Load Previous 10 Orders' dynamic query and 'Live Orders' tab. | Eliminates excessive vertical whitespace caused by uneven order lengths and ensures kitchen staff can focus on live tickets while retaining on-demand access to past orders. |
| **Universal Navigation Header (AppHeader)** | Deploy a standardized top header across all views featuring a clean home portal navigation icon, persistent active-role indicator, and integrated font size / high-contrast accessibility controls. | Eliminates navigation dead ends highlighted by Participant 2 by ensuring users can always return to the main portal from any screen without browser-level workarounds. |
| **Unified Design System & Palette Harmonization** | Centralize color tokens (#2E6F5E primary, #A7C957 secondary, #D9F0E1 accent), standardized typography, rounded card borders, and reusable button variants (btn-primary, btn-secondary, btn-outline) across Kiosk, Cashier, Kitchen, and Manager pages. | Resolves visual inconsistency and mismatched styling across windows reported by Participants 1, 4, and 5, establishing a cohesive, professional aesthetic. |
| **Accessibility Scale Bounds & Contrast Safeguards** | Implement strict boundary clamps on font resizing (minimum 80%, maximum 140%) and verify dark, high-contrast typography across all buttons (e.g., #142816 on light green backdrops). | Fixes the severe bug discovered by Participant 4 where text disappeared at minimum scale, and ensures compliant, readable contrast across all buttons. |
